## Composite-source rule

This option is a proposed textbook architecture, not a claim that one universal PhD in Measurement Sciences exists. `F2-67-009`, `F2-67-008`, and `F2-67-011` provide field-level standards for testing and research reporting. `F2-67-012` is a University of Maryland doctoral program exemplar and must remain labeled university-specific.

## Source-grounded construct

**SOURCE:** Responsible measurement requires evidence for validity, reliability / precision, fairness, accessibility, appropriate interpretation, professional responsibility, and consequences. Responsible education research requires transparent conduct and reporting. JARS adds observable reporting expectations for quantitative, qualitative, and mixed-method work. The UMD program provides one concrete doctoral pathway through measurement, quantitative methodology, comprehensive examination, specialization, and dissertation.

## Competency model

**INFERENCE:** An advanced measurement scientist can define a construct, design a defensible measurement or research system, evaluate evidence and uncertainty, examine fairness and consequences, communicate limitations, and improve the system without overstating what scores or models mean.

## Construct decomposition

1. Define construct, population, use, and decision consequences.
2. Select and justify designs, measures, data, and analytic models.
3. Build a validity argument with convergent, divergent, consequential, and boundary evidence as appropriate.
4. Analyze reliability / precision, uncertainty, missingness, bias, fairness, and accessibility.
5. Interpret results proportionally and communicate limitations.
6. Report methods, analyses, decisions, and deviations transparently.
7. Audit an evaluator or agent system for construct drift and unintended consequences.

## Evidence and task model

**CROSSWALK:** Research protocol, construct map, design memo, data / measure audit, analysis notebook, validity argument, fairness review, technical report, peer-review response, and improvement memo.  
**EVALUATOR DESIGN:** Require claim-to-evidence links, assumptions, uncertainty, alternative explanations, subgroup / accessibility checks, and explicit source provenance.

## Textbook spine

`Define → Design → Measure → Analyze → Validate → Interpret → Report → Audit consequences → Improve`

## Failure modes and constraints

- construct underdefinition;
- score treated as the construct;
- validity claim without evidence;
- precision confused with validity;
- fairness reduced to a single statistic;
- p-value or model output treated as a decision;
- university exemplar presented as field consensus;
- technical standard copied without context or rights review.
